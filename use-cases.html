The organisational loop

Six workflows, one governed learning system

Each use case can start on its own. The larger gain appears when verified learning moves between them: a support pattern changes onboarding, an incident changes a test, a contract constraint changes a public claim, and audience questions expose missing product knowledge.

Area What it contributes What it can improve next
Service desk Recurring questions, verified answers, escalation signals, and gaps in current guidance. Onboarding paths, customer commitments, product guidance, and engineering priorities.
Client relationships Approved commitments, decisions, acceptance evidence, operating context, and handover gaps. Delivery standards, support boundaries, incident priorities, and safe public promises.
Onboarding Retrieval failures, unclear ownership, conflicting sources, and questions experienced colleagues no longer notice. The source material, access model, and decision routes in every contributing area.
Outward communication Audience questions, objections, approved claims, corrections, and evidence about what people misunderstand. Product truth, discovery, support guidance, content planning, and prioritisation.
Development Implementation evidence, architecture decisions, enforced standards, verified solutions, and known limits. Handover, onboarding, support, incident prevention, and outward-facing claims.
Incidents Impact, evidence, confirmed cause, verified recovery, prevention work, and monitoring gaps. Runbooks, tests, design standards, customer communication, commitments, and training.

The gate matters as much as the connection. The source owner decides what is verified, removes customer-specific or restricted detail, chooses the receiving audience, preserves provenance, and sets a review condition. The private evidence may stay private while an approved conclusion becomes reusable.

How knowledge compounds without losing control

  1. Capture the useful outcome. Preserve the decision, evidence, procedure, or verified fix—not an unfiltered transcript of the work.
  2. Verify before promotion. Separate facts from hypotheses, identify the authoritative source, record the owner, and keep uncertainty visible.
  3. Publish to the right boundary. Create a sanitised, role-appropriate version in the permitted workspace; never use a link to bypass access rules.
  4. Retrieve it at the next decision. People and approved AI tools search, fetch the full source, and apply it where the next task actually happens.
  5. Return new evidence to the owner. Questions, contradictions, results, and exceptions become review signals that improve or retire the source.

The model is not one assistant with access to everything. It is a connected network of governed workspaces, owned knowledge, explicit permissions, and reviewable promotion paths.
